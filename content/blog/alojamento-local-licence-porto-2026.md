Alojamento Local, usually shortened to AL, is the registration that lets you rent a flat to holiday and city-break guests in Portugal. Between 2023 and 2026 the rules changed several times, so advice you read a year or two ago may no longer apply. This guide covers what an owner in Porto or Vila Nova de Gaia should check today.

## The national picture

National law now leaves most decisions to each municipality. Decreto-Lei 151/2026, published in July 2026, gives municipalities that do not yet have an AL regulation until 31 December 2026 to approve one. Where a municipality had suspended new registrations, that suspension can be extended only once and cannot run beyond 31 December 2026. These measures are not retroactive: valid registrations keep their status.

In practice this means the answer to "can I get a licence?" is local. The same flat could be registrable in one parish and not in the next.

## How Porto decides

Porto has its own municipal regulation for Alojamento Local. It divides the city's parishes into two groups:

- **Containment areas**, where the share of AL compared with housing is considered too high and new registrations are restricted.
- **Sustainable growth areas**, where new registrations are allowed within a maximum number per parish.

Guides published in 2026 do not agree on the exact thresholds that place a parish in each group, so do not rely on a number you read online, including in older articles that mention suspensions in the historic centre or Bonfim. Ask the Câmara Municipal do Porto for the current status of the specific parish, in writing if you are about to buy or renovate.

## Vila Nova de Gaia is a separate case

Gaia sits across the Douro from Porto's historic centre, but it is a different municipality with its own rules, its own tourist tax and its own decisions on AL. Treat a Gaia address as its own research task.

## What registration involves

The steps below are the same in spirit everywhere, but check the current form on the government portal:

1. **Prior communication (comunicação prévia)** through the online Balcão do Empreendedor on gov.pt, with the property and owner details.
2. **A waiting period** during which the council can oppose the registration. Sources describe a longer period in containment areas.
3. **Civil liability insurance** covering guests and third parties. Current guides cite a minimum of €75,000 per claim. Missing or expired insurance is a reason for cancellation.
4. **Equipment and documents in the flat**, such as a fire extinguisher, a fire blanket, a first aid kit and the complaints book. See [our checklist for getting a flat ready to rent](/blog/prepare-apartment-for-rental-porto).

## Before you buy or convert

- Confirm the parish status with the council, not with the seller or an advert.
- If the flat already has a registration, ask for the registration number and check it is valid and insured. Note that rules on transferring a registration with a sale have changed over time; confirm the current position with a lawyer.
- Read the condominium rules. Condominium decisions can affect AL in a building, and sources describe these rules differently, so a lawyer's reading of your building's regulation is worth paying for.
- If AL is not possible, consider medium or long-term letting. Our guide comparing the three models explains the trade-offs.
