Owners in Porto often start with the question "how much can I earn?". A better first question is "which kind of renting suits this flat and my plans?". Each model has a different balance of income, effort and flexibility.

## The three models at a glance

| Aspect | Short stays | Medium stays | Long-term lets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Typical guests | Holiday and city-break visitors | Remote workers, students, relocating families | Year-round tenants |
| Typical length | Nights to a couple of weeks | Weeks to months | A year or more |
| Licence | Alojamento Local registration | Fixed-term rental contract | Housing rental contract |
| Gross income | Highest potential, varies by season | Between the two | Lowest, most predictable |
| Running effort | High: guests, cleaning, linen, reviews, pricing | Medium | Low |
| Furnishing | Fully furnished and equipped | Furnished | Furnished or unfurnished |
| Flexibility to use or sell | High | Medium | Low during the contract |
| Income tax | Category B | Depends on the contract | Category F |

## Short stays

The appeal is income: a well-located, well-presented flat can earn more from short stays than from a tenant. The costs are real too. Booking platforms take a commission, there are cleaning and linen costs between every stay, utilities stay in your name, and the flat needs an accountant, insurance and an AL registration. Spaces' [income estimator](/#fees) shows how gross income, platform fees, management and running costs relate for T0 to T3 flats, based on Spaces' own projections.

Short stays only work if AL is possible for the address. Check that first; [our guide to AL licences in Porto](/blog/alojamento-local-licence-porto-2026) explains how.

## Medium stays

Medium stays suit flats near universities, hospitals and business areas, and owners who want income without the turnover of nightly guests. Guests stay longer, so there are fewer check-ins and cleans. They are made under a fixed-term rental contract rather than an AL registration, so the contract needs to be drafted properly. Ask a lawyer or your manager to use the right contract type for the purpose of the stay.

## Long-term lets

A long-term tenant gives steady income and the least work. In exchange you give up flexibility: Portuguese tenancy law protects tenants, and getting the flat back early is not simple. Long contracts can also benefit from a reduced income tax rate. See our [tax primer](/blog/rental-income-tax-portugal-owners).

## How to decide

1. **Check what is legally possible** for the address: AL status of the parish, condominium rules.
2. **Decide how long you want to keep the flat** and whether you want to use it yourself at times.
3. **Look at the flat honestly.** Short stays reward location, light and good design; a dated flat may need work first.
4. **Compare net income, not gross.** Subtract platform fees, management, cleaning, utilities, accountant, condominium, wear and tax.

If you are unsure, Spaces can give you a proposal with the stay type that fits and an income estimate for your apartment.
