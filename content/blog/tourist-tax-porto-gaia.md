Most guests barely notice the tourist tax. For an owner it matters more: you, or your manager, are the one who has to collect it and account for it to the municipality.

## The rates

| Rule | Porto | Vila Nova de Gaia |
|---|---|---|
| Rate | €3 per guest per night | €3 per guest per night (raised from €2.50 in 2026) |
| Maximum | 7 nights per stay | 7 consecutive nights |
| Age | Children under 13 are commonly listed as exempt | Applies to guests aged 16 and over |

Sources describe the age thresholds and other exemptions slightly differently, so confirm the current exemptions with each Câmara Municipal before you set up your listing. Gaia's published rules also mention exemptions for people with a disability of 60% or more and, with proof, for stays for medical treatment.

## How collection works

1. **Set it up in your listing.** Decide whether the platform collects the tax or whether it is collected separately, and make sure the guest sees it before booking.
2. **Collect it for every eligible guest night,** up to the seven-night cap.
3. **Declare and pay it to the municipality** on the schedule the Câmara sets, keeping records per booking.
4. **Reconcile monthly.** Mismatches between platform reports and what was paid are the most common problem, especially when a flat is listed on more than one platform.

## Why it matters

The tax is small per night but adds up across a year, and the obligation sits with the host. Getting it wrong means paying it yourself later. If a manager runs your bookings, ask exactly who collects and pays the tax and how it appears in your monthly statement.
